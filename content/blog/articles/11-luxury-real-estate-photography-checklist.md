Luxury real estate photography needs more than wide shots of every room. A strong shoot covers the hero exterior, twilight, views, signature details, lifestyle spaces and the neighborhood, all in high resolution with natural light. Plan the shot list with video in mind, and the same photos can become a cinematic listing video.

Use this checklist for your next shoot.

<div class="key-takeaways" markdown="1">
**Key takeaways**

- Prepare the home first. Styling matters more than equipment.
- Shoot in the best natural light, plus a twilight session.
- Capture details and lifestyle spaces, not just whole rooms.
- Deliver high-resolution files with no watermarks.
- Think in sequences. Photos that flow together make better video.

## How do you prepare a luxury home for photos?

Prepare the home by decluttering, styling key surfaces and fixing small details before the photographer arrives. Clear counters, remove personal items, open blinds, turn on every light, straighten towels and pillows and set the outdoor furniture.

A quick prep list:

- Hide cords, remotes, trash cans and toiletries.
- Add fresh flowers or greenery in a few spots.
- Make the beds with crisp, light linens.
- Turn on pool and landscape lighting for twilight.
- Move cars out of the driveway.

## What shots does a luxury listing need?

A luxury listing needs a complete set that tells the story of the home from arrival to evening. Here is our must-have list.

| Category | Shots |
|---|---|
| Exterior | Hero front, angled front, rear facade, aerial or high angle, entry and front door |
| Twilight | Front at dusk, pool and terrace at dusk, view at dusk |
| Living spaces | Great room wide, a second angle, dining, family room |
| Kitchen | Wide, island detail, appliances, butler's pantry |
| Primary suite | Bedroom wide, view from the bed, bath wide, closet |
| Other rooms | Each bedroom, office, gym, wine room, theater |
| Outdoor living | Pool, outdoor kitchen, terraces, garden, dock |
| Details | Fixtures, materials, art niches, fireplace, staircase |
| Views | From the main rooms and terraces |
| Neighborhood | Waterfront, parks, nearby landmarks |

## How do you light luxury real estate photos?

Use natural light wherever possible, shoot each side of the home when the sun is soft and add a twilight session for exteriors and views. Twilight images often become the most memorable photos in the set.

Avoid heavy filters and over-brightened skies. Buyers notice when photos don't match the home in person.

## What file specs work best for listing photos and video?

Ask your photographer for high-resolution JPEGs, no watermarks and consistent color editing. Landscape orientation is standard, but a few vertical shots of key spaces are useful for social media.

- High resolution (full-size files, not web-compressed).
- No watermarks or logos on the images.
- Consistent white balance across the set.
- A few vertical versions of hero spaces.
- Clear file names by room.

## How do you shoot photos that also work for video?

Shoot in sequences that follow how someone moves through the home: approach, entry, living space, kitchen, terrace, view. Include a few angles of each hero space and some close details. This gives video editors and AI tools natural "scenes" to work with.

When photos are going to become a cinematic video, every frame matters, because each scene is built from a real photo. We explain how scenes stay true to the photos in [AI listing videos and MLS rules](post:ai-listing-videos-mls-rules).

## Do you need drone photography for luxury listings?

Drone photos help when location is part of the value: waterfront, golf course, large lots or skyline views. Always use a licensed operator and follow local airspace rules.

## What are common luxury photography mistakes?

- Shooting before the home is styled.
- Only wide shots, no details.
- Over-edited skies and colors.
- Skipping twilight.
- Missing the view from inside.
- Delivering low-resolution or watermarked files.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many photos should a luxury listing have?

Enough to tell the full story, often 40 to 60 or more for large homes. Check your MLS photo limits and lead with the strongest images.

### Is twilight photography worth it?

For luxury homes, usually yes. Twilight shots show lighting, pools and views at their best and are often the most shared images.
