Prime property prices worldwide rose an average of 2.6% in the year to Q2 2026, according to Knight Frank's Prime Global Cities Index. Tokyo led by a wide margin at 50.7%, followed by Manila (14.6%) and Dubai (10.9%). In the U.S., San Francisco (5.0%) and Miami (3.9%) outperformed, Los Angeles grew 1.4% and New York dipped 0.4%.

Here is the full picture and why it matters for agents marketing luxury homes in the U.S.

## How are U.S. prime markets performing?

U.S. prime markets are mixed. San Francisco made the global top 10 with 5.0% growth, and Miami rose 3.9%, above the global average. Los Angeles grew 1.4%, below average, and New York fell 0.4%.

| U.S. city | Annual prime price change |
|---|---|
| San Francisco | +5.0% |
| Miami | +3.9% |
| Los Angeles | +1.4% |
| New York | -0.4% |

## What does "prime" property mean?

Prime property generally means the top tier of a city's housing market: the most desirable homes in the best locations. Knight Frank tracks these through local research partners, so "prime" is defined city by city rather than by one global price point.

## Why should U.S. agents care about global prime markets?
