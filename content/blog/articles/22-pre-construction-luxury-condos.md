Buying a pre-construction luxury condo means signing a contract on a unit before the building is finished, usually with staged deposits and the balance due at closing. The appeal is buying at today's price, choosing the best units and finishes and potentially gaining value by completion. The risks include construction delays, market changes, financing hurdles and a finished product that differs from the renderings.

Here is how it works and what to check. This is educational content, not financial or legal advice.

<div class="key-takeaways" markdown="1">

- You pay deposits over time and close when the building is complete.

## How does buying a pre-construction condo work?

You reserve a unit, sign a purchase contract and pay deposits in stages, often at signing, at certain construction milestones and at closing. The deposit structure varies by project and state. When the building is complete and receives approval to be occupied, you close and pay the balance, either in cash or with a mortgage.

## What are the benefits of pre-construction luxury condos?

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Early pricing | Prices often rise as a project sells and nears completion |
| Unit selection | Early buyers get the best views, floors and layouts |
| Customization | Finish and upgrade choices on some projects |
| Brand-new building | Modern amenities, systems and design |
| Time to plan | Deposits are spread out before closing |

## What are the risks?

The main risks are time and uncertainty. Construction can be delayed, the market can shift before you close, financing conditions can change and the finished building may differ from what you imagined.

Other risks to consider:

- **Developer risk:** financial strength and track record.
- **Contract terms:** what happens if the project is delayed or changed.
- **Assignment limits:** some contracts restrict reselling before closing.
- **HOA fees:** final fees may differ from early estimates.
- **Appraisal gaps:** if values change, financing may be affected.

## What should you check before buying pre-construction?

Use this checklist:

1. **Developer track record.** Completed projects, quality and timeliness.
2. **Deposit protection.** How deposits are held under your state's rules.
3. **Contract terms.** Delays, changes, cancellation and assignment rights.
4. **Specifications.** Finishes and features listed in writing, not just in renderings.
5. **Estimated HOA fees** and what they cover.
6. **Rental rules.** Whether short-term or long-term rentals are allowed.
7. **Financing.** Pre-approval and lender requirements for new condos.
8. **Location fundamentals.** Demand, views, access and future development nearby.

Always have a real estate attorney review the contract.

## How do agents market pre-construction projects?

Agents and developers market pre-construction with renderings, model units, sales galleries, video and lifestyle storytelling. Because buyers cannot walk through the finished home, cinematic video that shows the views, amenities and daily life is especially valuable. Keep marketing accurate and clearly label renderings as illustrations.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the deposit on a pre-construction condo?

It varies by project and location. Deposits are typically paid in stages, and the total can be a significant share of the price. Review the schedule in your contract.

### Can I sell a pre-construction condo before closing?

Sometimes, through an assignment, if the contract allows it. Many contracts limit or charge fees for assignments, so check before you buy.

### What happens if construction is delayed?

Your contract sets out what happens. Some allow extensions, others give buyers certain rights after long delays. An attorney can explain your options.

### Do I need a real estate agent for pre-construction?

It is a good idea. An agent can help compare projects, negotiate terms and represent your interests, often at no direct cost to the buyer in new developments.
