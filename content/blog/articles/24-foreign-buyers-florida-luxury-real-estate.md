Yes, foreign buyers can generally purchase real estate in Florida, including luxury homes and condos. The process is similar to a domestic purchase, but international buyers should plan for financing differences, U.S. tax obligations, FIRPTA withholding when they sell and possibly specific state rules depending on their country. A local agent, real estate attorney and cross-border tax advisor are essential.

Here are the basics. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

## Can foreigners buy property in Florida?

In general, yes. Foreign nationals can buy homes, condos and land in Florida. However, Florida has adopted restrictions on property purchases by individuals and entities connected to certain countries, and rules can change. Ask a Florida real estate attorney to confirm what applies to you before you make an offer.

## How does the buying process work?

The process typically follows these steps:

1. **Assemble your team:** agent, attorney, tax advisor, lender if financing.
2. **Define the search:** location, budget, property type, how you will use it.
3. **Tour, often remotely first.** Video tours and cinematic listing videos help international buyers shortlist homes.
4. **Make an offer** and sign a purchase contract.
5. **Due diligence:** inspections, title search, condo or HOA documents.
6. **Funding:** wire funds or finalize financing.
7. **Closing:** sign documents (sometimes remotely) and receive the deed.

## Can foreign buyers get a mortgage in Florida?

Some lenders offer loans to foreign nationals, often with larger down payments and more documentation than for U.S. residents. Many international luxury buyers purchase with cash. Talk to lenders that specialize in foreign national loans.

## What taxes do foreign buyers pay?

Owners pay annual property taxes like any other owner. If you rent the property out, U.S. tax rules on rental income may apply. Florida does not have a state personal income tax, but federal taxes still apply. Your home country may also tax the income or gain.

## What is FIRPTA?

FIRPTA (the Foreign Investment in Real Property Tax Act) generally requires the buyer to withhold a portion of the sale price when a foreign person sells U.S. real estate, commonly 15% of the gross sales price, with some exceptions and reduced rates. The withholding is applied against the seller's U.S. tax liability. Plan for it before you buy, and ask a tax advisor how it applies to you.

## Should you buy in your own name or through a company?

It depends on taxes, privacy, estate planning and liability. Some buyers use a U.S. or foreign entity, others buy personally. Each option has different tax effects, so get professional advice before closing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do foreigners pay more taxes on Florida property?

Annual property taxes are generally the same, but foreign owners may face different rules on rental income and must plan for FIRPTA withholding when they sell. A tax advisor can explain your situation.

### Can I buy a Florida home without visiting?

Yes, many international buyers do, using video tours, remote inspections and remote closings. Still, seeing the area in person is recommended when possible.

### Does buying property give me a U.S. visa?

Buying a home does not by itself grant immigration status. Speak with an immigration attorney about your options.

### Do I need a U.S. bank account?

It can make paying taxes, insurance and utilities easier. Requirements vary by bank.
