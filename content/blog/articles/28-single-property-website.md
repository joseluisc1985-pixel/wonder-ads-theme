A single property website is a dedicated page or site for one listing, often on its own address like 123OceanDrive.com. For luxury homes, it should open with a cinematic video, then tell the home's story with a photo gallery, key details, floor plans, neighborhood highlights and a simple form to request a private showing.

Here is what to include and how to use it.

<div class="key-takeaways" markdown="1">
**Key takeaways**

- A property website gives every ad, post and brochure one clean destination.
- Lead with the cinematic video, then the story, then the details.
- Keep the inquiry form short: name, email, phone, preferred time.
- Track visits and inquiries so you can report results to sellers.
- It also impresses sellers when you present your marketing plan.
</div>

## Why do luxury listings need a single property website?

Portal pages are crowded with ads, other listings and distractions. A dedicated page focuses entirely on one home and gives buyers the full experience. It also gives you control: your branding, your story and your inquiry form.

It's the perfect destination for paid ads. See our [Facebook ads guide for luxury listings](post:facebook-ads-for-luxury-listings).

## What should a luxury property website include?

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hero video | Sets the mood in seconds |
| Headline and short story | Names what makes the home rare |
| Photo gallery | Full set in high resolution |
| Key details | Price, beds, baths, size, lot, features |
| Floor plans | Helps serious buyers picture the layout |
| Neighborhood | Lifestyle, schools, dining, commute |
| Agent details | Your photo, name and contact |
| Inquiry form | Request a private showing |

Keep the page fast and easy to use on a phone, where most visitors will see it.

## How do you write the story section?

Write two or three short paragraphs about life in the home, then list the facts. Lead with moments and feelings, follow with features. Our guide to [writing luxury listing descriptions](post:luxury-listing-description-examples) has examples.

## Should a property website have its own domain?

A custom address like the street name is memorable and looks premium on signs and brochures. A page on your own website works too and builds your site over time. Many agents use a custom domain that points to a page on their main site.

## How do you drive traffic to a property website?

Link it everywhere: social posts, paid ads, email announcements, the brochure QR code and your listing presentation follow-up. Use the cinematic video in ads and send viewers to the page to learn more.

## What should you track?

Track visits, video plays, time on page and inquiries. Share a simple weekly report with sellers. It shows the marketing is working and builds trust during the listing period.

## Do single property websites help SEO?

They can rank for the address and building name, which helps when buyers search for a specific home. The bigger value is giving every campaign a focused destination that converts visitors into showing requests.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a single property website cost?

It depends on the tool. Many templates and listing platforms offer property pages at low cost, and custom designs cost more. The video and photos usually matter more than the design.

### Should the price be on the property website?

Usually yes. Buyers expect it, and it saves time for everyone. Some ultra-luxury listings use "price upon request," but that can reduce inquiries.

### What happens to the site after the home sells?

Mark it as sold and keep it as part of your portfolio, or redirect it to your website. Sold pages show future sellers how you present homes.
