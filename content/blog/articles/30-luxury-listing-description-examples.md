A strong luxury listing description opens with what makes the home rare, describes two or three moments of life there, then lists the key facts clearly. Keep it specific and calm. Swap clichés like "stunning" and "entertainer's dream" for concrete details buyers can picture.

Here is the structure, plus examples you can adapt.

<div class="key-takeaways" markdown="1">
**Key takeaways**

- Lead with the one thing that makes the home rare.
- Describe moments, not adjectives.
- Follow the story with clear, scannable facts.
- Cut clichés and vague superlatives.
- Check your MLS rules and fair housing guidelines before publishing.
</div>

## What makes a luxury listing description effective?

It's effective when a buyer can picture living there after reading it. Specific details do that better than adjectives. "Watch the sunrise over the bay from your bed" says more than "breathtaking views."

## What structure should a luxury listing description follow?

1. **The hook.** One sentence on what makes the home rare.
2. **The story.** Two or three sentences about life in the home.
3. **The details.** Key features and specs, easy to scan.
4. **The location.** What's nearby and why it matters.
5. **The next step.** How to see it.

## Example 1: Waterfront home

**Before:** "Stunning waterfront home with breathtaking views. Entertainer's dream with pool and dock. Must see!"

**After:** "Set on 70 feet of open bay, this home starts every day with the water. Coffee on the terrace, the boat ready at the private dock, long lunches by the pool and evenings watching the skyline light up across the bay. Six bedrooms, seven baths, a chef's kitchen and a primary suite that opens to the view."

## Example 2: Penthouse

**Before:** "Luxurious penthouse with amazing city views and top-of-the-line finishes."

**After:** "Above the city, everything slows down. Floor-to-ceiling glass wraps the living room in light, a 1,200-square-foot terrace hosts dinner under the skyline and a private elevator opens directly into the residence."

## Example 3: Family estate

**Before:** "Gorgeous estate perfect for families. Lots of space for entertaining."

**After:** "Built for family life at every scale. Weekend breakfasts around the island, kids racing to the pool, a guest house for visiting grandparents and a lawn big enough for the whole neighborhood. Minutes from top schools and the club."

## Which words should you avoid in luxury listings?

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Stunning, breathtaking, amazing | The specific view or feature |
| Entertainer's dream | "Seats 12 on the terrace, steps from the outdoor kitchen" |
| Must see! | A clear next step |
| Luxurious finishes | Name the materials |
| Perfect for families | Describe the spaces, not the buyer |

That last one matters. Describe the property, not who should live there, to stay in line with fair housing guidelines.

## How long should a luxury listing description be?

Long enough to tell the story and cover the key facts, within your MLS character limit. On your property website and brochure, you can go longer. See our guide to [single property websites](post:single-property-website-luxury-listing).

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a luxury listing description mention the price?

The price usually appears in its own field on the MLS and portals. In the description, focus on story and features.

### Can I use AI to write listing descriptions?

AI can help with a first draft. Always check every fact against the home, remove clichés and make sure it follows MLS and fair housing rules.

### How many features should I list?

Lead with the three or four that make the home rare, then list the rest briefly. Too many features in a row become a blur.

### Who should write the description?

The agent who knows the home best, ideally after asking the sellers what they love most about living there.
