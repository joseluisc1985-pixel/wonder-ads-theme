Real staging is usually better for luxury homes that buyers will tour in person, because the home looks the same at the showing as it does online. Virtual staging is faster and cheaper, and works well for vacant rooms, pre-construction or a quick refresh, as long as images are clearly labeled. Many agents combine the two: physically stage the main rooms and virtually stage secondary spaces.

Here is how they compare.

## What is the difference between virtual and real staging?

Real staging places actual furniture and decor in the home. Virtual staging adds furniture and decor digitally to photos of empty or dated rooms. Both aim to help buyers picture the space, but only real staging changes what buyers see in person.

## How do virtual and real staging compare?

| | Real staging | Virtual staging |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Higher, often monthly rental | Lower, per image |
| Speed | Days to schedule and install | Often within days |
| In-person experience | Matches the photos | Rooms are empty at the showing |
| Flexibility | Fixed style | Different styles per image |
| Best for | Occupied or vacant luxury homes buyers will tour | Vacant rooms, pre-construction, quick refreshes |
| Disclosure | Not required | Usually required by MLS rules |

## When is real staging the better choice?

Choose real staging for the main living spaces of a luxury home that buyers will visit, especially at higher price points. When buyers walk into the same beautiful rooms they saw online, the home feels true to its marketing. Our [luxury home staging tips](post:luxury-home-staging-tips) cover where to focus.

## When does virtual staging make sense?

Virtual staging makes sense for vacant secondary rooms, homes where installing furniture is impractical, pre-construction units and quick updates to older photos. It's also helpful for showing different uses of a flexible room, like an office or gym.

## What are the rules for virtual staging?

Most MLSs require virtually staged images to be clearly labeled, and many also ask agents to include an unstaged version or a note in the remarks. Rules vary, so check your MLS and brokerage policy. Never use virtual staging to hide defects or change permanent features like floors, walls or views.

## Can you use virtually staged photos in a listing video?

Yes, if the disclosure stays clear. Any video made from virtually staged photos should keep the labels visible and avoid implying the furniture comes with the home.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is virtual staging worth it for luxury homes?

It can be for vacant secondary rooms or pre-construction. For the main spaces of a home buyers will tour, real staging usually creates a better experience.

### Do buyers mind virtual staging?

Most buyers are used to it when it's clearly labeled. Problems arise when images are misleading or the gap between photos and reality feels too big.

### Can I virtually remove furniture?

Virtual decluttering is common but is still an alteration. Follow your MLS rules on disclosure and never remove permanent features.

### What's the best approach for a large vacant estate?

Many agents physically stage the main living areas and primary suite, then virtually stage secondary rooms with clear labels.
