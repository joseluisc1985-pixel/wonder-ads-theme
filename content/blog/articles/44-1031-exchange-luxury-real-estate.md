A 1031 exchange lets a U.S. real estate investor defer capital gains tax by selling an investment property and reinvesting the proceeds in another "like-kind" investment property. The key rules: both properties must be held for investment or business use, you must identify replacement property within 45 days and close within 180 days, and a qualified intermediary must hold the funds.

Here are the basics for luxury investors. This is educational content, not tax advice.

<div class="key-takeaways" markdown="1">
**Key takeaways**

- A 1031 exchange defers, but does not eliminate, capital gains tax.
- It applies to investment and business property, not your primary residence.
- Identify replacement property within 45 days of selling.
- Complete the purchase within 180 days.
- Use a qualified intermediary and a tax professional.

## What is a 1031 exchange?

It's a provision in Section 1031 of the U.S. tax code that allows investors to defer capital gains tax when they exchange one investment property for another of like kind. In real estate, "like kind" is broad: most investment real estate can be exchanged for other investment real estate in the U.S.

## Can you use a 1031 exchange for a luxury home?

Only if the property is held for investment or business use, such as a rental. A primary residence doesn't qualify. Vacation homes are a gray area with specific rules about personal use and rental history, so get professional advice.

## What are the 1031 exchange deadlines?

| Deadline | Rule |
|---|---|
| 45 days | Identify potential replacement properties in writing |
| 180 days | Close on the replacement property |

Both clocks start when you sell the original property and run at the same time. Missing either deadline can make the exchange fail.

## What is a qualified intermediary?

A qualified intermediary is an independent party who holds the sale proceeds and uses them to buy the replacement property. If you receive the money yourself, the exchange can be disqualified.

## How do investors use 1031 exchanges to trade up?

Many investors use exchanges to move from smaller properties into higher-value ones, such as a portfolio of rentals into a luxury rental home or a property in a faster-growing market. To defer all of the gain, the replacement property generally needs to be of equal or greater value and use all the proceeds.

## What are common 1031 exchange mistakes?

- Touching the sale proceeds instead of using an intermediary.
- Missing the 45-day identification deadline.
- Buying a replacement that's worth less, leaving taxable "boot."
- Trying to exchange a primary residence.
- Not planning the replacement purchase before selling.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a 1031 exchange eliminate taxes?

No. It defers capital gains tax until you sell without exchanging. Tax planning can affect what happens later, so talk to a tax professional.

### Can I exchange a rental for a vacation home?

It depends on how the vacation home is used. Personal use limits apply. Get advice before buying.

### Can foreign investors use 1031 exchanges?

Rules can be more complex for foreign sellers, including FIRPTA withholding. See [foreign buyers in Florida luxury real estate](post:foreign-buyers-florida-luxury-real-estate).

### Do I need an attorney for a 1031 exchange?

You'll need a qualified intermediary, and a tax professional and real estate attorney are strongly recommended.
