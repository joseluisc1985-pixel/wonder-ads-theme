Some luxury buyers purchase homes through a limited liability company (LLC) for privacy, liability protection or estate planning. The trade-offs include more complex financing, extra costs, possible tax effects and, for a primary residence, the risk of losing certain owner benefits. Whether it makes sense depends on how you'll use the property, so speak with a real estate attorney and tax advisor first.

Here are the main pros and cons. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

<div class="key-takeaways" markdown="1">
**Key takeaways**

- LLCs can add privacy and separate a property's liabilities from your other assets.
- Financing is harder. Many residential loans require ownership in your own name.
- Taxes and owner benefits can change when an LLC owns the home.
- Ownership disclosure rules have been changing. Check current requirements.
- Get legal and tax advice before you choose a structure.
</div>

## Why do luxury buyers use LLCs?

The most common reasons are privacy, keeping your name off public property records, and liability, separating risks tied to the property from your personal assets. LLCs can also help with estate planning and shared ownership among family members or partners.

## What are the pros and cons?

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| More privacy on public records | Harder to get a standard mortgage |
| Can help separate liability | Formation and annual fees |
| Useful for shared or family ownership | Possible tax and insurance changes |
| Can simplify transfers in some cases | Owner benefits for primary residences may be affected |
| Common for investment properties | More paperwork and compliance |

## Can you get a mortgage if an LLC buys the home?

It's harder. Many residential mortgages require the buyer to be an individual. Some lenders offer commercial or portfolio loans to LLCs, often with different terms. Cash buyers have more flexibility.

## Does an LLC fully protect your privacy?

Not completely. Ownership disclosure rules for companies and real estate purchases have been changing at the federal and state level, and title companies may need to know who owns the LLC. An attorney can explain what's currently required.

## Is an LLC better for an investment property or a primary home?

LLCs are more common for investment properties and rentals, where liability separation matters. For a primary residence, owning through an LLC can affect benefits tied to living in your own home. Discuss your plans with your advisors. See [second home vs. investment property](post:second-home-vs-investment-property).

## What should you ask your attorney?

- Will an LLC achieve my privacy and liability goals here?
- How will it affect financing, insurance and taxes?
- What are the formation and ongoing costs?
- What disclosure rules apply?
- How should the LLC be set up and maintained to keep its protections?

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it legal to buy a house through an LLC?

Yes, it's a common structure, especially for investment properties. Make sure it's set up properly and complies with current rules.

### Do I save on taxes by using an LLC?

Not necessarily. It depends on your situation, how the LLC is taxed and how you use the property.

### Can foreign buyers use an LLC?

Many do, but there are added tax and reporting considerations. See [foreign buyers in Florida luxury real estate](post:foreign-buyers-florida-luxury-real-estate).

### Can I move a home I already own into an LLC?

It's possible but can affect your mortgage, insurance and taxes. Get advice first.
